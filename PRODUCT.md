## Register

brand

## Users

- **Gift buyers (primary, web):** 25–55, on their phone, arriving from an emotional reaction video on TikTok/Meta or a gift-intent search. Two weeks (or two days) before someone's moment — a birthday, an anniversary, or just something they need to say. Emotional, slightly rushed, skeptical of AI gimmicks until they hear a preview.
- **Recipients (any platform):** open a link someone who loves them sent. They must be able to hear the song instantly in any browser, no install, no account. The claim into the app ("keep it forever") comes after the tears, never before.
- **Creators (iOS app, later Android):** buyers who installed the app as their song studio; repeat senders. The app is the keepsake shelf and the repeat engine, not the storefront.

Verified usage truth (prod, 2026-07): top occasions are _i love you_, _celebration_, _thank you_, _encouragement_ — ahead of birthdays. People use Porizo to say something to a person, not to fill calendar slots.

## Product Purpose

Porizo turns one real memory into an original song with the recipient's name in it, in minutes. It is a **gift you buy on the web, delivered as a link that plays anywhere, kept forever in the app** — priced at the personalized-gift point (~$20), not the app-utility point. Success = a purchased song → a recipient who cries → a recipient who claims it → a recipient who answers with a song of their own (the loop).

## Brand Personality

Warm, heartfelt, effortless. Physical anchors: a handwritten card with a pressed flower inside; warm lamplight at a kitchen table; the pause before someone presses play in front of the person the song is for. The voice speaks feelings, not features: "Some feelings are too big for a card."

Named references: Songfinch's emotional gravity (without the $199 formality and 7-day wait), Wonderbly's giftable warmth, Apple's product-page restraint for the demo moments.

## Anti-references

- **Voice-clone promises** (giftsong.net's lane) — we do not have the tech; never position on "your voice." Hard ban.
- **AI-tool aesthetics** (Suno-style prompt boxes, generator UI, "credits", model names) — this is a gift, not a toy.
- **Cheap-AI gimmick look** — sparkle emojis, robot iconography, neon gradients, "AI magic" copy.
- **Fake urgency** — share links are lifetime; no countdown timers on the gift itself. (Occasion dates are real urgency; use those.)
- **Hard price claims in ad creative** — storefront pricing varies.
- **Velvet & Gold** (the pre-April-2026 dark luxe theme) — deprecated; Warm Canvas v2 is the identity.

## Design Principles

1. **The reaction is the product.** Every surface builds toward the moment the recipient hears their name sung. Design for the person watching someone listen.
2. **Preview before pay.** Our structural edge is hearing the song before buying (competitors deliver in days, blind). Never bury the preview; never gate it behind an account.
3. **One memory at the heart.** Message-first: the recipient's name and one real memory anchor everything. Fewer, warmer inputs beat configurability.
4. **The gift moment is sacred.** A recipient hitting a wall (install prompt, signup form, error) at the moment of the gift is the worst failure the product can have. Play first, ask later.
5. **Keepsake gravity.** A song has a home, not a download folder. The app is where songs live; the web is where they're given.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA on all web surfaces (contrast ≥4.5:1 body, ≥3:1 large text — Warm Canvas ink ramp is pre-verified for the warm background).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all motion, including the player's waveform/progress animation.
- Audio-first product: always pair audio with visible lyrics; player controls keyboard-operable with visible focus.
- Dynamic Type (iOS) / rem-based fluid type (web); touch targets ≥44px.
