Council Verdict

Where the council agrees

The free web player satisfies the recipient's entire need: hear the gift. Binding then offers nothing they don't already have (the link is lifetime; they can replay forever). 1.7% is not a bug; it's the arithmetic of a multi-gate funnel guarding a prize the user already holds.

  • The PIN (buried in the sender's message) and the forced Apple sign-in are the two most destructive, least-justified gates. Both fire before any value, and both fail silently (logged as generic "unbound").
  • The recipient's strongest impulse is to reciprocate ("make one back"), not to "claim." The product optimizes the wrong verb.

Where the council clashes

Build a web create/buy flow (the Expansionist's storefront, $1.99, no app) vs. don't: 4 of 5 reviewers flagged it collides with Apple's IAP rules and is effectively a second product. Resolved against the pure web-storefront for now.

Optimize the funnel now (Executor) vs. validate the loop converts at all first (Contrarian). The chairman sides with the Contrarian as the gating step, then the Executor's fixes.

Blind spots the peer round caught

  • 605 is probably inflated. iMessage/WhatsApp auto-fetch link previews, the sender re-checking, and bots all count as web_player_opened. The real human denominator is unknown.
  • An unknown share of views are desktop/web, structurally unable to bind (platform != "web" hard block).
  • Nobody is re-engaging the sender, the one proven buyer. "Your gift was played 4×" is the warmest re-purchase trigger you have.
  • The funnel is uninstrumented, so we cannot see which 401 dominates.
The Recommendation

Diagnose before building. The numbers are too small and too suspect to optimize blind. Then ship the cheap, high-leverage fixes that move the gate after the emotional payoff: pre-fill the PIN through the deep link, defer sign-in until after the song is saved to the device, and reframe the CTA from "claim your song" to "make one back."

The One Thing To Do First

Run the diagnostic SQL on production before writing any product code: (1) segment the 605 by device type and dedupe link-preview hits, (2) check whether any recipient ever became a sender, (3) count which claim error (SIGN_IN_REQUIRED vs INVALID_PIN vs no-attempt) dominates.

Full advisor responses
The Contrarian — "polishing the lock on an empty vault"
The mechanism isn't broken — the design is. The web player is too good: it plays the song free, instantly, zero friction. The recipient's entire need is satisfied in the browser, so binding offers nothing they don't already have (it's lifetime — they can replay forever). Stack the friction: install → Apple sign-in → find PIN buried in a text → 14-day handoff token. Four cliffs, each halves survivors. The PIN is the assassin — it lives in the sender's message; if forwarded/screenshotted/scrolled past, binding is impossible and you log it as generic failure. You're probably bleeding people at INVALID_PIN. Hard question you're avoiding: is "bound" even the right metric? Senders are the buyers. Has a single recipient of 605 ever become a sender? If zero did, fixing binding to 50 changes nothing. Prove the loop converts at all before optimizing the funnel.
The First Principles Thinker — "you're optimizing the wrong verb"
You're asking why recipients don't bind. Wrong question. You designed a claim flow and accidentally measured friction. The viral loop needs recipients to become senders, not to "bind tokens." A recipient who loved the song wants to make one back. Three assumptions to kill: (1) recipients shouldn't claim the sender's song at all — it's not theirs; go web player → "Make one back" → app. (2) The PIN protects nothing worth 98% of your funnel — delete it. (3) "Free listen in browser" isn't a feature, it's the leak; the web player should be a trailer, not the whole movie. Real question: what makes a recipient send?
The Expansionist — "the 605 are screaming to buy, not to claim"
The web player isn't a leak — it's the most undervalued asset Porizo owns. 605 warm humans, each handed a personalized song made for someone they love, who sat through it and felt something — that's 60× the entire paid base. The asset isn't the library slot, it's the intent to reciprocate. (1) Highest-converting next action: "make one back, right now, on this same web page" — pick occasion, type a name, hear a 15s preview, pay $1.99. The player becomes a storefront. (2) Every play is a testimonial — capture a reaction / "send one back" referral that re-engages the sender too. (3) Drop binding as the conversion goal entirely. [Council caveat: a pure web pay flow collides with Apple's IAP rules and is a second product — treat as a later bet, not a Monday fix.]
The Outsider — "there's no reason left to do the hard part"
You hand me a link, I tap it, the song plays. Done. My friend made me a thing, I heard it, I felt it, I sent back a heart emoji. Why keep going? You lose me the moment "Save to Library" throws me at the App Store — downloading an app is a big ask, and signing in with Apple ID makes it feel like signing up, not receiving a gift. A 6-digit PIN from up in the text? I've lost that text in the thread; I'm not scrolling back. You already gave me the good part for free. What would move me: let me save it without an account first (text/email me the link to keep), pre-fill the PIN so I never type anything, and give me a reason about ME — "make one back for them" beats "claim your song." Biggest fix: kill the forced app download to keep something I already own.
The Executor — "Monday: pre-fill the PIN, instrument the funnel"
The web player is where 605 views go to die. Attack it first. Monday task: change the song-end state to a hard wall — "This song was made for YOU. Claim it free, keep it forever" — with the PIN pre-filled into the deep link. Then in priority order: (1) Pre-fill the PIN — it's in the share message already; pass it through the OneLink so claim views auto-populate. One day; server already accepts it. (2) Soften SIGN_IN_REQUIRED — bind to the device first, prompt sign-in to "save permanently" after. (3) Instrument the funnel — log OneLink click, app open, claim attempt, which 401 fired. Do #1 and #3 this week. Don't touch the token TTLs — not the bottleneck.
Peer review highlights

Strongest (unanimous, 5/5): the Contrarian. Only response that refuses to optimize before validating the premise with a falsifiable test ("has a single recipient become a sender?").

Biggest blind spot (4/5): the Expansionist. The web storefront abandons the iOS distribution surface, collides with App Store IAP / the 30%, and assumes buy-intent with zero evidence.

What everyone missed: (1) the data itself — 605 views likely inflated by iMessage/WhatsApp link previews, the sender, and bots; (2) the device-type split (web views can never bind); (3) sender re-engagement — the proven buyer, ignored by all; (4) the simplest test: just ask a few recipients why they stopped.
